Poplar.agency · active planning baseline

Make local
feel connected.

A working plan for turning neighborhood marketing into a measurable, repeatable service—using real businesses as clearly labeled case-study examples.

Concept status
Dots and Dashes, Chicago Sugar Daddy, Momo Factory, and Lakeview Elegant Salon are public examples. Campaigns, offers, partnerships, results, and endorsements are hypothetical until confirmed.

The outcome

Build a credible demonstration of Poplar.agency as a neighborhood customer-development partner—not merely a social-media service, discount marketplace, or crypto product.

Discover→Visit→Collect / record→Return→Cross-visit→Refer

Four workstreams

Evidence

Research each business and candidate Atmosphere component. Every claim is labeled VERIFIEDINFERENCEQUESTION.

Campaign

Design one connected neighborhood loop with distinct merchant roles, customer entry points, offer logic, and success measures.

Operations

Specify onboarding, staff actions, hardware, software, consent, support, reporting, and what Poplar does versus the merchant.

Artifacts

Produce a campaign website, customer flow, merchant/staff view, pitch deck, sample creative, and report mockup. The prototype must be useful for a sales conversation and honest about what is not live.

Internal system

Maintain an Obsidian wiki, Kanban board, decision log, research library, campaign templates, and weekly review tools.

Architecture posture

Front office

  • Campaign and merchant pages
  • QR-first, NFC-enhanced entry
  • Mobile passport or membership view
  • Events and routes
  • Consent-based follow-up

Merchant operations

  • Slow-period diagnosis
  • Margin-safe offer rules
  • Simple staff verification
  • Content and event activation
  • Monthly outcomes report

Back office

  • Merchant CRM
  • Campaign registry
  • Redemption ledger
  • Evidence library
  • Support and incident log

Build principle: start with ordinary web, QR, a private operational ledger, and manual verification. Add Wallet passes, credentials, secure NFC, AT records, or deeper integrations only when validated behavior justifies the complexity.

Agent council

Main Hermes

Orchestration, architecture-of-record, budget, secrets, final QA, user decisions, and verified Git integration.

Hermes sub-agent

Approved-ticket implementation: research packages, website and tool builds, wiki/Kanban scaffolding, documentation, and tests in isolated workspaces.

DeepSeek V4 Flash

Cost-efficient source discovery, case-study and Atmosphere research, structured extraction, comparison matrices, and first-pass alternatives.

Low-cost utility / QA

Only bounded triage, tagging, cleanup, checklist audits, regression-test generation, and concise defect reports under a hard task cap.

Cost and stopping rule

Before an expensive action: define the smallest useful step, forecast cost, reduce scope and turns, prefer deterministic scripts, batch independent questions, reuse evidence, and stop when acceptance criteria pass. No agent is used for formatting or file enumeration when a script can do it.

  • Every handoff has an objective and output contract.
  • Unsupported claims become explicit gaps.
  • Sub-agent self-reports are independently verified.
  • Public and private customer data stay separate.
  • Changes remain reversible and versioned.
  • Further iteration stops when it would be cosmetic.
